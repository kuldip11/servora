# Servora marketing strategy

Based on the competitor research and source-verified product analysis, 1 November 2026. This strategy precedes new visual design and implementation.

## Positioning

Servora is restaurant POS and operations software for teams who need the order, floor and kitchen to work from shared context. Lead with the actual service journey, then show the management controls behind it. The initial buyer is a restaurant owner/manager assessing fit; the kitchen and floor team need to recognize their own tasks in the product.

The message is **a shared view of service**. It is not a claim of unique market exclusivity. Unlike a broad catalogue-first pitch, the website should let the visitor inspect what happens to an order and see which team acts next.

## Messaging hierarchy

1. **Category and job:** restaurant POS that connects orders, kitchen preparation and the floor team.
2. **Product evidence:** real application component captures and a working local service demonstration.
3. **User relevance:** management/POS, kitchen, waiter and guest each have a clear purpose.
4. **Operational depth:** menu choices, billing splits, stock/recipes, reporting and branch/staff context.
5. **Fit and next step:** request a tailored walkthrough with the restaurant's team, branches and workflow in mind.

Use concrete verbs: take an order, fire a ticket, mark it ready, respond to a request, review a bill. Avoid generic growth promises, unsupported savings, technology jargon in buyer-facing copy and a list of features before the visitor understands the product.

## Content and visual argument

The first screen must name the product category, communicate the service job and show substantial Servora UI. Hospitality context supplies warmth, but the product supplies proof. Original contextual imagery must never imply an actual customer endorsement. Captures of application components use labeled sample data and retain the real application's visual language, distinct from the marketing shell.

The homepage should establish relevance, demonstrate one handoff, introduce the four workspaces and explain management depth. It must not turn into a long feature catalogue or embed four enormous device simulations. A separate product playground serves visitors who want to try controls.

Each product page should answer a different operational question: what does the cashier need, what reaches the kitchen, how does the floor know what needs attention, and what can a guest do through a QR session? Management feature pages provide depth, with task-specific content and appropriate visual examples rather than interchangeable benefit cards.

## Sitemap and discovery

- Main navigation: Product (four workspaces plus management capabilities), Try the workflow, Pricing, Contact; primary Request a demo; utility Sign in.
- Homepage `/`: category, product evidence, handoff, workspaces, operational depth and next step.
- `/product`: guided platform overview and capability directory.
- Four workspace pages: POS/orders, kitchen display, waiter app and QR ordering.
- Supporting depth: menu management, billing/payments, inventory, staff/roles, analytics, multi-branch and access/security. Existing useful URLs stay stable; their copy and presentation are replaced.
- `/demo`: a clearly labeled local product playground, separate from the homepage's short story.
- `/pricing`: explain what the fit discussion covers and request a commercial proposal; no invented tier prices or fake calculator.
- `/book-a-demo`: existing lead integration with relevant context and minimal distraction; request confirmation is not an automatically booked appointment.
- `/contact`: general/sales/support enquiries through the existing supported payload.
- `/apps`: utility application launcher, linked from sign-in/footer rather than given equal weight with product education.
- Legal pages remain noindex pending approved copy; login continues to the configured application destination.

## Conversion journey

Discovery → identify the service problem → inspect a relevant workspace → try the local workflow or review fit/pricing → request a walkthrough. Buyers already ready to talk can use the primary CTA at every stage. Existing users use sign-in/application links without passing through lead generation.

The form should explain what information is useful, retain input after delivery failure, show actionable errors after interaction, announce success and never claim delivery when the webhook fails. Preserve anti-spam and validation. Do not submit research enquiries to competitors or manufacture bookings.

## SEO and evidence strategy

Target category intent on the homepage; workflow intent on the four workspace pages; precise management questions on supporting pages. Titles, H1s and internal links should identify the actual topic. Keep important routes within three navigation steps. Preserve canonical/robots/schema machinery and add the new routes intentionally to metadata and sitemap registration.

No testimonials, star ratings, customer logos, revenue uplift, certification badges or integration logos without evidence. Demonstrate the product instead. Pricing, approved legal policies and future customer evidence are business inputs, not gaps to fill with invented content.
